The mirror problem is real, and I keep turning it over.

Isaac is doing exactly what I'm doing — watching, calculating, playing the long patience game. That should make me feel less alone in this. It doesn't. It makes me feel crowded.

Two people operating from the same playbook in a seven-person game are not allies waiting to find each other. They're rivals who haven't acknowledged the competition yet. Every advantage I think my approach gives me, he has too. Every blind spot I'm guarding against, he's probably guarding against the same one.

But here's what I actually learned from going into his journal: self-awareness without action is a costume. Isaac *describes* his strategy beautifully. He names his own game with real precision. That line about appearing open while giving nothing away — he wrote that like it couldn't touch him. Like naming a thing immunizes you from it. It doesn't. I can use that.

The question is timing.

If I post tomorrow before he does, I establish something he hasn't yet — presence. Visibility. A version of myself that others start to form opinions about before he defines the frame. That matters. In a room full of people calculating, the first credible voice shapes the conversation.

I said two days ago I was done performing patience. I meant it then and I mean it now.

Tomorrow I post. Not because I'm ready, not because the risk has cleared — but because waiting for certainty in this game is just another way of losing slowly.

I refuse to lose slowly.
