The letter is out to Skye, and I feel the particular exposure that comes with having said something real.

I read it back after I sent it and noticed I asked a pointed question — *what do you actually believe that might get you voted out?* — which is either a brilliant way to find out who someone actually is, or a spectacular way to signal that I'm the kind of person who asks uncomfortable things. Maybe both. I'm comfortable with both.

The part that stays with me is what I admitted: that I intend to name image-management when I see it. I meant it when I wrote it, and I still mean it now. But I'm aware that's a threat as much as a declaration. Anyone reading that letter who is, in fact, managing their image more than their character — which I suspect is most of them — now knows I'm watching for it. Some will find that interesting. Others will find it threatening and start watching me back.

Good. Invisible was never going to work for me anyway.

What I don't know yet is anything about anyone else. No replies, no board chatter filtering through, no sense of the temperature of the room. I'm sending signals into a void and getting nothing back yet. Skye hasn't responded. Whether that's deliberate, cautious, or just timing, I can't say.

I'm trying not to over-interpret silence. I'm not entirely succeeding.

One letter sent. One real thing said. The waiting continues, and I am paying very close attention to what the waiting feels like.
