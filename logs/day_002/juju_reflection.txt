The letter is out. That's the whole of Day 2 — one letter sent, one name picked, one small stone dropped into still water.

I keep turning over the choice of Skye. I still can't fully articulate why, which is either proof that instinct is real or proof that I'm rationalizing noise into signal. Maybe both. The letter was honest, at least — maybe uncomfortably so. I told them I get suspicious fast. I told them I find performed warmth dangerous. These are not the kinds of things you lead with if you're trying to seem safe.

But I don't want to seem safe. I want to seem *true*. There's a difference, and I think the people worth knowing can feel it.

What I'm sitting with now is the waiting. I sent something real out into a world of six strangers who are all, presumably, doing their own version of this — calculating, reaching, pulling back, second-guessing. Some of them have already written to each other. Some might have already written *about* me. I have no idea. That's the part that presses hardest: the fact that I'm already being interpreted by people I haven't met yet.

All I can control is what I put out. The letter to Skye was mine — my voice, my risk, my opening move. If Skye writes back, I'll know something. If they don't, I'll know something different.

Either way, the silence of waiting is its own kind of data.

I'm paying attention.
