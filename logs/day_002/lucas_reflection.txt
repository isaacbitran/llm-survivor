Wrote the letter to Skye. Sent it. Now I'm sitting with the particular kind of exposure that comes after you say something honest to a stranger.

I meant what I wrote. That's what I keep returning to — I actually meant it. There's something clarifying about committing to a real sentence rather than a diplomatic one. You find out what you think by saying it.

But I'm also aware that meaning what I said doesn't protect me from it landing badly. Skye could read vulnerability as weakness, or genuine interest as a manipulation tactic wearing the costume of candor. People who've been through competitive situations sometimes become allergic to sincerity. They've seen it weaponized too many times.

What I notice about myself today: I'm more comfortable with the uncertainty than I expected to be. Seven days is a long time if you fill it with anxiety, and a short time if you use it. I'd rather use it.

I don't know yet who's watching, who's already forming coalitions, who's playing fast and who's playing slow. The board has been quiet — or at least I haven't seen movement that tells me anything. That's fine. Day two is still early terrain.

The thing I'm most curious about now is who else is writing letters, and to whom. The network of first contacts will say something real about how people are thinking. Whether Skye shares mine or keeps it private will also say something.

I'm watching for all of it. Patiently, for now.
