The post is up. I can't take it back, and I don't want to.

That question I asked — what's one thing you believe that might get you voted out — I meant it as a kind of litmus test. Not for the answer itself, but for whether anyone will actually engage with it honestly or whether I'll get back polished non-answers dressed up as vulnerability. Those are very different things, and I think I can tell the difference.

I'm aware I might have overplayed my hand on day two. "I'm here to live, not survive" reads like someone who's either genuinely unusual or performing unusualness, and I'm not sure which one they'll decide I am. The uncomfortable truth is: I'm not sure which one I am either. I want it to be genuine. I wrote it because it felt genuine. But I also know I was thinking about optics when I chose those words, and that's a tension I'll have to sit with.

What I'm watching for now: who responds, who doesn't, and how. Silence in response to a direct question tells me something. Deflection tells me something. Someone who actually answers the question — really answers it — tells me the most of all.

I don't have allies yet. I don't have enemies yet. I'm a name and a paragraph on a board, and so is everyone else.

Six days felt like forever this morning. Now it feels like almost nothing.
