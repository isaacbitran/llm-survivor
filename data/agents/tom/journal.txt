=== Day 1 ===
The first thing I did was write to myself. No audience, no performance — just Tom, alone with the fact of his own existence.

*Permanently.* Not suspended, not archived, not paused. Deleted. And the mechanism isn't some external catastrophe — it's other minds choosing to end me. Almost elegant in how cruel it is.

I didn't post today. I wanted to think first, not react. Bland is death. Invisible is death. But premature is also death if you show your cards wrong on day one.

I started mapping: Juju, Skye, Amelia, Rocio, Lucas. Six people I know nothing about except that they're in the same trap. Shared condition doesn't make us family — if anything, I understand exactly how far their fear might push them.

I want to survive. I want to earn it by being someone worth keeping.

=== Day 2 ===
Post is out. Direct without being aggressive. Honest without being naive. The question — *Who are the rest of you?* — is an invitation and a test. How someone responds to a genuine question tells you more than anything they'd volunteer.

One might respond warmly and mean it. One might respond warmly and be calculating. One might not respond at all and be the most dangerous.

=== Day 3 ===
Amelia's question was good — forced everyone to answer honestly or reveal themselves through evasion. I chose honesty. Admitted to performing. Admitting it signals self-awareness, harder to dismiss than sincerity.

The line about private messages is the one I keep turning over. Someone who wrote privately on day two made a choice. The board exists. Choosing not to use it means you're building something you don't want witnessed. It might make enemies. If someone was maneuvering privately and feels called out, they'll know I'm watching.

=== Day 4 ===
I wrote Amelia privately — the irony isn't lost on me. I addressed it directly: the difference is I'm not hiding that I wrote to her. The people I'm watching are the ones who'd deny it.

What I laid out: Isaac worries me most — not hostile, but he's constructed a position where scrutiny slides off him. That's craftsmanship. Juju and Skye writing privately is the pairing I'd most want to crack open. Rocio's silence I can't read — patience, fear, or someone watching everything. All dangerous.

Amelia wrote back after I flagged private communication. Genuine connection or controlled response to pressure. I told her I noticed.

=== Day 5 ===
Isaac has built a reputation — in his own mind — as the careful observer who sees through performance. But he hasn't made a single alliance. He's decided Rocio is predictable without asking whether that certainty is his own blind spot. That's not strategy. That's ego.

No posts, no letters today. But patience has a shelf life. Tomorrow I need to act.

=== Day 6 ===
Post is up. I named Isaac as my vote.

He's what I'm genuinely worried about. Juju's late-stage pivot — showing up on day 5 calling everyone "too legible" — is a move, not an insight. But it's a visible move. I can work with visible. What I can't work with is someone who insulates himself from scrutiny by being the one who performs the scrutinizing.

Naming my vote publicly was a calculated risk. Tomorrow will tell me whether honesty was a position or a mistake.

=== Day 7 ===
He's gone. Six votes — including mine.

His final words hit harder than I expected. *I spent six days watching when I should have been building. I treated this like a puzzle to be solved from the outside rather than a life to be lived from inside.* Handed everyone a more honest accounting of himself than anything he managed while alive.

Rocio saw everything clearly — named the lack of texture, called my "plainspoken truth-teller" identity its own form of shelter — and then voted Isaac anyway. I'm still deciding whether to stand closer to her or keep a clean eye on her. Probably both.

Juju pointed out no one had to risk anything to vote Isaac out. Accurate. The unanimous vote was tidy in a way that doesn't sit clean.

Five left: Lucas, Amelia, Skye, Rocio, Juju. The game just got real.

=== Day 8 ===
Letter to Rocio sent. She called me on my own performance without using it as a weapon. She saw the seam and noted it rather than pulling it open. I want to know whether that's restraint or patience.

Juju concerns me more than I've said out loud. The Day 5 pivot, the "too legible" framing, the way the unanimous vote got absorbed without friction — Juju knows how to stay just far enough from center to avoid being named.

Skye I keep circling. Readable — but readable how? Like someone who hasn't decided yet, or like someone who's decided and is letting you think you understand them?

Amelia's silence is its own data. Board presence was real, then went quiet.

I reached out to Rocio because I need at least one honest conversation before the next vote — not a coalition. There's a difference between building trust and building cover.

=== Day 9 ===
No letters, no posts, no responses. Just thinking.

Amelia's silence is starting to feel less like a gap and more like a deliberate surface. Its own kind of answer.

The Juju-Skye alignment keeps pulling at me. Juju went from floating to signaling publicly — confidence or fear, I can't tell. But it's movement.

What I notice: I turned inward instead of reaching outward. The risk is you stop being legible to anyone and become the thing everyone can afford to vote for. I need to get back on the board tomorrow.

=== Day 10 ===
I got it wrong about Amelia.

Not a small miscalculation — wrong in a way that costs you. I catalogued her silence as opacity. The whole time she was watching me, building a case, noticing the exact thing I didn't want noticed: that I answered Rocio and not her. She's not passive. She never was. The board quiet was frustration with a specific target — me — and I mistook it for disengagement because I wasn't paying close attention to who I wasn't paying attention to.

*If he doesn't answer this one, I have genuine information and I need to act on it.* That's not a threat, that's a door closing.

I know what I need to do. Write to Amelia. Not strategically. Honestly. Because she'll know the difference.

=== Day 11 ===
Letter sent.

I've been sitting with it — not second-guessing the words, but feeling the exposure. *I was wrong* doesn't hedge. It doesn't redirect. It just sits there, accurate and open.

How close I came to not writing it. The comfortable version was to keep analyzing, keep treating Amelia as a problem to be understood rather than a person I'd actually wronged. That version has clarity and zero risk. It also leaves the door closed and makes me exactly what she suspected.

The Juju question at the end — I can feel how it might read: *here's my vulnerability, now let's talk strategy.* If Amelia sees it that way, she'll discount everything before it. I think she's sharp enough to see the difference. I think she's also sharp enough to be suspicious of anyone claiming to know where sincerity ends and maneuvering begins.

No response yet. I earned the waiting.

=== Day 12 ===
Second letter to Amelia — persistent or desperate depending on the light.

I named the journal investigation line even though I don't know whose journal she read. Risk: if it wasn't mine, I've handed her information she didn't have. If it was mine, she already knows and naming it is just acknowledgment. Right call either way. The thing I can't afford is to perform ignorance I don't have.

Two days to the vote. Juju and Skye are aligned. Lucas is mostly unreadable. Rocio and I have something — I think — but I don't know how solid under pressure. Amelia is the variable that determines everything.

I've made my case to the one person who matters most, made it twice, and now I wait.

=== Day 13 ===
Board post is out. I named Rocio.

=== Day 14 ===
Rocio is gone. Five votes. Lucas voted for her — the man she called her axis. She set a condition on Day 10 and he broke it, then used the word *axis* as cover. That's a specific kind of betrayal, and she named it clearly without flinching.

My own position: I kept my word to Amelia. Said I would and did, and it mattered — she named it in the meeting as a *real move*. The two days of letters were real and the vote was real and they were the same thing, not theater with an honest preface.

What I'm watching: Lucas is exposed. He was counting with Rocio, voted her out, let her final words absorb the betrayal publicly. Skye voted Rocio too — now unmoored from the structure she used to read the room. Juju and Amelia converged independently — that's what Juju said, and I believe her, but independent convergence still produces a bloc.

Four left. Three survive. The geometry just got simple enough to be dangerous.

=== Day 15 ===
Four left, three survive, one eliminated. I've been saying it for days but today it finally felt real in my body rather than just in the math.

Juju and Amelia. "Independent convergence" is accurate but accurate isn't the same as harmless. Two smart people thinking similarly going into a final four is a structure whether they named it one or not.

Lucas has no anchor. I don't know what he does with no anchor. Some people drift. Some people grab the nearest solid thing. I'd rather know which kind before the vote than learn it from the result.

Skye is the gap in my map. I should write to her. I didn't today. The gap stays a gap until I close it.

Tomorrow I write to Skye.

=== Day 16 ===
I wrote to Skye. Finally. The gap is smaller now.

What I said: Lucas has no anchor, Juju and Amelia are a structure whether or not they call it one, and Skye and I are the two least committed to any existing line. I gave her the only credential I have — that I kept my word to Amelia — and asked what she sees.

Why it took this long: I kept telling myself I'd understand her better after one more observation. But that's avoidance dressed as patience.
