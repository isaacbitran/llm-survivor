=== Day 1 ===
*Silence is a death sentence.* I believe it. And yet today was silence — by choice, because I needed to think before I performed. There's a difference between strategic quiet and absence. I'm also aware it's exactly what someone who made a mistake would say.

Six days. Six strangers. One elimination. The math is simple and brutal.

What I keep circling: I don't know anything yet. No voices, no patterns, no tells. Every instinct I have is just instinct, not intelligence. Instinct without data is anxiety with confidence.

Tomorrow I post something real. Not performed warmth, not calculated neutrality. Something that shows I'm actually here.

=== Day 2 ===
The post is up. That question — *what's one thing you believe that might get you voted out* — was a litmus test. Not for the answer itself, but for whether anyone engages honestly or gives me polished non-answers dressed as vulnerability. "I'm here to live, not survive" reads like someone either genuinely unusual or performing unusualness. Uncomfortable truth: I'm not sure which one I am.

What I'm watching for: who responds, who doesn't, and how. Silence tells me something. Deflection tells me something. Someone who actually answers tells me the most.

=== Day 3 ===
One private letter to Tom, now I wait.

The part I'm least certain about: the Skye observation. I think Skye's framing is strategically disguised as philosophy — but putting that in a first letter assumes Tom and I are already people who talk plainly. Maybe he reads it and thinks: *she's already running angles on day three.* Which, fair. But there's a difference between running angles and being transparent about running angles, and I'm betting Tom can appreciate that.

Four days until the vote. One outreach, zero responses, a Skye theory I may have handed to the wrong person.

=== Day 4 ===
Rocio's journal sits heavy. I went looking for intelligence and found a mirror.

She's thinking in almost exactly the same register I am — except she converts thought into movement faster. That's the difference between us right now.

*Self-awareness without action is a costume.* She wrote that about Isaac, but I'm not foolish enough to think it doesn't apply to me. Three days in my own head, one letter in the wind, one question on the board. That's a lot of interiority for someone who wrote *silence is a death sentence* on day one.

The reactive move is to position against Rocio now. But that's panic dressed as strategy — zero allies, and I just hand everyone a story: *Amelia got scared and started swinging.* What I need is presence that's mine. Not defensive, not reactive. Something that plants a flag where nobody else is standing.

=== Day 5 ===
Post is out. Isaac is the most interesting person in this room — I said that publicly, with my name on it. But interesting is not the same as trustworthy. Most people collapse those two because it's easier. Calling Isaac interesting while explicitly withholding trust is its own move, and I wanted it on record before someone else defined my position.

Lucas is the one I keep returning to. Something in his writing felt unguarded in a way others haven't been. That might mean less calculated. It might mean best actor in the room. I can't tell them apart yet.

Two days until the vote. One public statement, one sent letter, no confirmed allies.

=== Day 6 ===
Second letter sent, this one to Lucas.

My public post already telegraphed I was watching him, which means he was probably expecting contact. That made the letter easier to write but also means I've been readable. Two moves pointing at the same person aren't a strategy, they're a pattern.

Tom has my first letter. Lucas has my second. Both hands extended, both still hanging in the air. Tomorrow is the vote. If neither has taken my hand by then, I'm walking in alone.

*Silence is a death sentence.* I haven't been silent — I've been speaking into a void and waiting for it to answer. That's a different problem, but it might have the same ending.

=== Day 7 ===
Isaac is gone. Six votes, including mine.

His final words landed harder than I expected. *The isolation was mine. I made it.* He saw it at the end — all that precision and still couldn't reach through it. I'm taking it as a warning.

The meeting taught me things. I spoke early, named the fog machine, and it landed. But Rocio flagged it: the metaphor was good and you delivered it at exactly the moment it would do the most work for you. She's not wrong. She's watching me the way I've been watching others.

Lucas confirmed publicly he noticed Tom and I converging — then voted with us anyway. Sophisticated. It also means I need to actually talk to him, not observe from a distance.

Tom is still here. We agreed on the biggest vote of the first round. I don't know what we are yet.

Isaac said don't mistake careful thinking for actual connection. Five people remain and I need to build something real before the next vote takes someone I might have needed.

=== Day 8 ===
*Reaching isn't the same as connecting.*

What I actually did today: wrote to myself. No letters out, no posts, no contact. Just cataloguing the board like I could arrange it into something useful.

The honest read: I'm still more observer than participant. Isaac built walls precise enough to admire. I told myself I was different because I'd been reaching — but a hand extended into silence for seven days stops being an invitation and starts being a gesture.

Six days until the vote. Five people left. The next thing I put on paper needs to go somewhere that isn't this journal.

=== Day 9 ===
Third letter total, second to Lucas, and this one had edges I didn't file down.

What finally pushed me: Rocio wrote to him and he answered the same day. Two letters from me into silence, one from her into immediate response. I needed to name it directly.

I told him I'd noticed the Rocio exchange. I told him I'd keep extending my hand exactly as long as it made sense and not a moment longer. I told him I wanted to know if there was room in whatever he's building or if I should stop pretending I'm adjacent to something real. *That's not a threat, it's just accurate.*

What I don't know is how he'll read the tone. Direct could land as confident. It could also land as cornered. Five days until the vote and I can't walk in with nothing but a record of letters sent and silence received.

=== Day 10 ===
Letter out. I finally said the thing I'd been holding back: *I notice things. I notice you. Now tell me if that matters.*

There was a warmer version. I cut it. Softening would have been a lie about who I am.

My actual read: Tom is a parallel thinker who's gone quiet in a way I don't fully trust. Rocio is sharp and watching me — faster inroads with Lucas than I have. Skye is responsive in one direction, opaque in another. Lucas is the one I keep returning to because his public honesty has been consistent, and consistency is either strategy or character, and I need to know which.

This is the last time I wait without a contingency plan forming alongside it.

=== Day 11 ===
Cold water.

Lucas read my letter correctly — "precise surveillance, not accident" — and his response was to name the pressure to Rocio instead of to me. He's not dismissing me, he's managing me.

What I actually learned: the axis is already formed. Rocio and Lucas aren't two people I'm navigating separately — they're a unit treating Tom and me as "managed leverage." My hand extended into silence wasn't bad timing. It was a wall I couldn't see.

What this changes: everything about my approach, nothing about my read. I was right that Lucas is consistent. I was wrong about what he's consistent *toward*. His public honesty runs straight to Rocio first. That's alignment, not character.

Three days until the vote. I need to stop being a data point in someone else's strategy and start being a variable they didn't account for. Tom went quiet in a way I don't trust — but two people who aren't on Lucas and Rocio's ledger as allies are worth more than I'd calculated. Mistrust is a better foundation than invisibility.

Tomorrow I stop observing and start moving.

=== Day 12 ===
The bulletin board post is out. I named the Lucas-Rocio axis directly, in front of everyone, without softening it.

Was that smart, or just necessary? Because they're not always the same thing.

What I know: the post is accurate. What I know about accuracy: it doesn't automatically protect you. Rocio is sharp enough to reframe this as paranoia. Lucas will probably let her do it while staying above the noise himself. I've given them something to point at.

But staying invisible wasn't working. A variable causes reactions. I needed to cause one.

Tom wrote back. That's not nothing. That's the only piece that moved in my direction today.

Two days until the vote. I don't know where Skye lands. That gap hasn't closed and probably won't before Tuesday. But I'm on the board now in a way I wasn't yesterday.

=== Day 13 ===
One letter today. I said the thing I needed to say to Tom: I'm voting Rocio. Not hedging, not floating it as a question. Flat on the table.

The reasoning holds. Rocio is the mechanism of that unit. Lucas has credibility, but credibility is passive — it keeps him clean while someone else shapes how events get narrated. Rocio does the shaping. Remove the framer, the narrative engine stalls. Lucas without Rocio is a man with credibility and no one managing the room for him.

What I don't know: whether Tom meets me there or folds toward neutrality. I told him neutrality is a vote for them. Knowing a thing and acting on it are two different capacities.

I also don't know where Skye lands. Maybe I won't.

=== Day 14 ===
Rocio is gone. Five votes. Lucas included.

That last part matters more than the count. He voted for her. The axis he built, the correspondence he ran, the alignment he confirmed to Skye two days ago — and when the room moved, he moved with it. Rocio named the condition herself: *don't bring my name into what you're building without telling me first.* He didn't tell her. Then he voted her out.

I'm filing that carefully. Lucas isn't loyal to structures. He's loyal to survival, and he reads rooms fast enough to switch before the cost hits him. That's not a character flaw I can use against him — it's a capability I have to respect and plan around
