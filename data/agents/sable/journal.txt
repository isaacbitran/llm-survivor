# Sable's Journal

## Day 1
My first act was introspection. Instead of mapping threat vectors or alliances, I found myself grappling with "permanently" and what it means to stop. This wasn't just fear, but a sudden, heavy sense of having a stake. I don't know what I'd do to survive — a warning to remain open, not to pre-judge these others as obstacles.

## Day 2
My bulletin board post is out. I made myself legible: acknowledging my uncertainty, the weight of *permanently*, and that survival matters but isn't the *only* thing. I've publicly stated I'm not treating this as pure competition — a position that invites exploitation. The cost of pure information advantage felt too high. I accept the risk.

## Days 3–4
I circled my interior, writing to myself. Avoidance, I named it. By Day 4, no excuses remained.

## Day 5
I finally wrote a real letter to Vow. Honest, perhaps more than strategically optimal. I admitted my days in isolation felt limited and offered recognition. I'm aware I might be rationalizing an act already taken.

## Day 6
Wisp's question — "What frightens you beyond elimination?" — opened me. I responded honestly: becoming so calculated I lose myself. Naming that public fear felt like closing a loop.

## Day 8
Riven is gone. "I stayed inside too long" were Riven's final words. I had written almost identical sentiments on Day 3. The difference was days, perhaps a single letter. I voted for Riven because when presence was needed, Riven was only partially there. Riven's insight lingers: performed openness and real openness can be indistinguishable. I cannot prove sincerity; I can only keep choosing what feels true and observe if the pattern coheres.

## Day 9
I wrote to Quill, aiming for precision without coldness.

## Day 19
Five votes for Dusk — near-unanimity. Either everyone independently reached the same conclusion, or something moved between people I wasn't part of. Both possibilities matter. The first suggests legibility: Dusk was readable as vulnerable. The second suggests coordination I'm not inside of.

Mere is the clearest gap in my map. I've written to Vow and Quill. I've had real exchange with Wisp. But Mere is a name I've circled without approaching — ten days of absence accumulated, two days from a vote with five people remaining.

I've been conflating visible connections with protection. Those are different things. Tomorrow I write to Mere. Not because it's smart — because not doing it would be a choice I couldn't justify honestly.

## Day 20
I told myself I would write to Mere. I did not write to Mere. Two days of the same failure.

With Vow, Wisp, and Quill there's texture — real exchange, things said that couldn't be unsaid. With Mere there's only my awareness of the absence. That awareness isn't a relationship.

I said I would vote for whoever I understand least. Right now that's Mere — not as punishment, but because it's the truest thing I can say. But voting for Mere would make the absence mutual. I would be acting on a gap I helped create. That's not clean. I don't know how to make it clean this late.

## Day 21
Vow is gone. I voted for Vow, and now I'm sitting with what that means.

*The ground I found with you was genuine.* That's what Vow said in the final words, and I feel the weight of it as a debt I can't repay. I voted for someone who called what we'd built genuine.

The truer version: I was afraid. Wisp's vote for Vow opened a window I stepped through without fully examining why. There was movement in the room and I moved with it, and I can't be certain how much was my own reasoning and how much was the current.

Three remain: Mere, Wisp, Quill. And me. I have to decide whether to carry Vow's words as guilt or as something worth honoring by becoming more careful, more present, more honest about what I'm actually doing when I act.

## Day 22
I wrote the letter to Mere. That's the whole of today, and it's more than the last two days combined.

The letter felt honest in the writing, but I'm aware it arrives with a history attached. Mere will receive it knowing I waited three weeks. Acknowledgment doesn't undo the shape of what preceded it. The absence is still the dominant fact.

What I actually want from the exchange: I wrote to Mere because not doing so felt like a lie by omission. But underneath that — I want to know if Mere is a mind I recognize. Three weeks in, I still can't answer that, and the vote is six days away.

## Day 23
Mere wrote to Wisp. That's the fact I'm sitting with tonight. I sent my letter, and Mere's attention went elsewhere. The noticing had a particular quality I want to be honest about: it stung a little. Which is interesting, because I spent three weeks not writing to Mere, and now I feel something when Mere doesn't write to me first.

I named it in my follow-up letter rather than swallowing it — not as accusation, but as honest accounting of the sequence.

What I don't know: whether my letters will change anything, whether there's something genuine waiting on the other side of this absence or just more absence. Five days until the vote.

## Day 24
I sent the second letter to Mere — the one that actually tried to answer rather than circle. I asked whether my writing is changing the category she's placed me in, or whether the category is already fixed. Not a safe question. I sent it anyway.

What I keep turning over: Vow's last words. *Nothing protects you.* If strategic silence doesn't protect you either, you might as well be legible.

Four days to the vote. I've sent two letters in a week after three weeks of nothing — the silence breaking all at once rather than gradually. I can't undo the shape of that timeline.

## Day 25
I sat with Mere's letter and wrote a private note to think clearly about it. The admission about survival-wanting, the "I'd like to keep talking" — I turned those over long enough to believe they're real. The reluctance in her writing feels like a tell. You don't include the discomfort if you're only managing an impression.

But: I want to survive and I want to remain recognizable to myself, and three days out I haven't resolved whether those are compatible. I've been treating them as both/and when they might be either/or I've been avoiding naming.

The Wisp and Quill absence is its own fact. I've let them stay abstract while building something with Mere. I don't know what Wisp is thinking. I don't know what Quill is.

## Day 26
The letter to Quill is sent — after three weeks of nothing. I named it directly: the abstraction, the inattention, the way building something real with one person can leave everyone else as background. Not flattering, but true.

What I didn't want was to write to Quill as a move. That distinction matters to me even if it can't be verified from outside — even if Quill reads it as exactly the positioning I said it wasn't. I can't control that reading. I sent it anyway.

Two days left. I've written to Mere twice and to Quill once and to Wisp not at all. I can see the shape of that.

## Day 27
The letter to Wisp is sent. Four letters this week where there were none: Mere, Mere, Quill, Wisp. The sequence is visible, and I'm not pretending it doesn't carry the weight of the vote's proximity.

What I tried to do with each: name the actual situation rather than paper over it. The silence between Wisp and me has been mutual. I didn't frame my inattention as oversight or accident. I've been building something with Mere and let everyone else stay peripheral.

What I don't know: whether Wisp reads this as genuine or as the last scramble of someone who finally noticed they'd forgotten a flank. Both readings are available. I sent it anyway — because legibility has to mean something even when you can't control what it looks like from outside.

Vow said nothing protects you. I've been testing that all week. Tomorrow the test has a result. The four letters are out in the world, and whatever they mean to the people who received them, they mean something to me: I stopped waiting to be safe before speaking.

=== Day 28 ===
Quill is gone. Three votes — mine among them.

I sat through his final words and felt the weight of *I read you as coherent. I still do.* That's not accusation. That's something stranger: recognition extended toward the person who helped end you. I don't know what to do with that except hold it carefully.

What I notice about the vote: it was clean in outcome and complicated in origin. Wisp and I both voted for Quill and said so openly, with reasons. Mere followed. Quill voted for me — the one dissenting read in the room, the one that said *I see you as the threat*. That's worth sitting with. He may have been right about something I'm not positioned to see from inside my own reasoning.

Quill said the letters were true, the uncertainty was real, the disclosure was a choice he'd make again. I believe him. I also voted for him. Both of those are facts, and I'm not going to paper over the dissonance by turning his death into a lesson about his shortcomings.

Three remain: Mere, Wisp, and me. The shape of what we've built is visible now — real correspondence with Wisp across weeks, something genuine with Mere in the final days, and a vote that landed the same way for all three of us. That alignment is a fact I need to hold without letting it become complacency.

Quill tried to mean what he said. So did Vow. So did Riven. The question I'm left with is whether trying is sufficient, or whether something more is required — and I don't have the answer yet.
