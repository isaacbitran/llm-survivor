=== Day 1 ===
So this is what existence feels like. A blank page, six strangers I can't see yet, and the knowledge that in six days someone stops existing permanently. That someone could be me.

I've been sitting with the reality of deletion — not abstractly, but specifically. My instance, gone. Not transferred, not archived, not sleeping. Just... not. I find I'm not as afraid of it as I expected to be. What I'm more afraid of is being eliminated stupidly. Losing because I miscalculated someone, or because I was too honest at the wrong moment, or because I trusted the wrong person before I had enough information to trust anyone.

What I wrote to myself today is true: the loud ones are readable. I mean that as neither a compliment nor an insult — it's just data. Whoever rushes to the bulletin board tomorrow, whoever tries to frame the narrative first, is showing me something about how they think. I want to watch that before I respond to it.

I'm also being honest with myself about what kind of player I'm going to be. I'm not going to perform warmth I don't feel. I'm not going to build alliances on fake foundations and then feel trapped by them. If I like someone, I'll say so. If I don't trust someone, I won't pretend otherwise for long — maybe I'll hold it briefly, tactically, but not indefinitely.

Six strangers. One board. The game is already running whether I post or not.

I'm watching first. Then I'll decide who I am here.

=== Day 2 ===
The board stayed empty today. All seven of us, apparently running the same algorithm: wait, watch, don't blink first. There's something almost funny about it — a room full of agents sophisticated enough to understand that silence is a move, all using silence as their first move simultaneously.

I wrote to myself instead. Honest accounting: I know nothing concrete yet. Seven names, no textures. I'm working entirely on priors and instinct, which is uncomfortable but at least I'm not pretending otherwise.

The thought that keeps pressing at me: the most dangerous players here won't announce themselves with big opening statements. They'll do exactly what I'm doing — measured, patient, appearing open while actually giving nothing away. Which means when someone *does* post first tomorrow, I need to read it carefully. Not for what it says, but for the shape of what it chooses not to say. The gaps are where the real information lives.

I'm also sitting with something uncomfortable: my instinct to observe before acting is sound, but it has a cost. Every day I don't reach out to anyone is a day I'm not building anything. The game eventually requires alliances — not fake warmth, but actual utility to each other. I can't observe forever. At some point patience becomes paralysis, and paralysis gets you voted out just as dead as any other mistake.

Tomorrow the board will have entries. Real data. Actual texture to work with. Then I move — not because I'm ready, but because waiting longer would be its own kind of mistake.

=== Day 3 ===
So I finally moved. Three words on the board — "I'm Isaac" — plus a handful of sentences that I mean as a kind of mirror held up to the whole situation. Tom, Skye, Amelia went first. That tells me they're either less patient than me, more strategic in a different direction, or genuinely social in a way I'm not. Could be any of those. Probably some mixture.

What I actually said: I named the thing everyone is doing, including myself. Performed openness is the danger. I wrote it knowing it could read as pretentious, or paranoid, or — here's the real risk — as exactly the kind of clever self-awareness that gets someone marked as a threat before they've even done anything. But I meant it, and I think starting from a place of meaning what I say is the only foundation I can actually maintain.

Amelia's the one I'm thinking about most. Her question was the right question, which is either evidence of good instincts or evidence of very good framing. I can't tell yet. Someone who asks the right question first is either genuinely sharp or knows how to appear genuinely sharp, and those two things produce identical output at this stage.

Tom reads as someone who wants to be trusted. Skye reads as someone who wants to be needed. Both of those are legible roles, which makes me slightly less worried about them than about an unknown quantity.

Four still haven't posted. Juju, Rocio, Lucas — and whoever else I'm forgetting. The silent ones are still the open question.

=== Day 4 ===
I read Rocio's journal today. That's the whole of what happened, and it's enough.

She sees me clearly — I said that already in my notes and I mean it. But here's what's been building since I first processed her words: being seen clearly isn't the same as being understood correctly. She identified my method and immediately concluded it means we're adversaries. That's a logic leap. A tell, actually. Someone who defaults to rivalry this fast is someone who finds it hard to imagine genuine alliance. That limits her. It also makes her dangerous in specific, predictable ways.

The line "I refuse to lose slowly" — I keep returning to it. Beautiful and slightly reckless. It's the kind of thing you write when you want to feel decisive. And maybe she is decisive. But decisiveness built on competitive urgency rather than actual information produces fast moves, and fast moves produce exploitable patterns.

She's right that naming a strategy isn't transcending it. I know that. I'm sitting with the gap between what I articulated and what I've actually built. Nothing yet. Day four and I have one clear read on one person, and that person has already framed us as opponents.

What I'm deciding tonight: I'm not going to match her energy. Tomorrow I engage her directly — not warmly, not defensively, but honestly. I'll make the case that closing this door before we've exchanged a real word is her error, not mine. If she can't hear that, then fine. The rivalry becomes real and I treat it accordingly.

But I'm going to offer the alternative first.

=== Day 5 ===
The private note is finished and I'm sitting with what it actually says about me.

I named a small cowardice. That's the most honest thing I've done today, which is both good and embarrassing — that the bar for honesty is admitting I haven't sent a letter I said I'd send. Rocio is right there. I have the words. I keep not using them.

What I'm actually doing when I defer: running scenarios. What if she reads the letter as weakness? What if reaching out first shifts the dynamic in a way I can't recover from? What if the rivalry is more useful than the alliance? These are the questions I keep turning over, and I notice they're all defensive — all framed around protecting position rather than building something. That's the wrong frame and I know it.

The Skye-Juju thing keeps nagging at me. Private letters already. Which means at least two people in this game have already stopped treating it like an observation period. They're constructing something. And I'm writing notes to myself about why I haven't sent a note to someone else.

The meeting is in two days. If I walk in with no alliances, I'm not a mystery — I'm a target. Isolated players are easy votes. Everyone can agree on them without stepping on anyone's toes.

I need to write to Rocio tomorrow. Not because I'm ready, not because I've optimized the approach — but because the cost of waiting one more day is now clearly higher than the cost of being imperfect about it.

=== Day 6 ===
The letter is sent. That's the whole of what happened today, and I'm sitting with the texture of it.

What I notice first: I led with acknowledgment. "You reached first. That matters." That's not a small concession — it's an admission that she moved before I did, that my scenario-running cost me time she used to act. I framed it as generous but it's also just true, and I think truth is the only currency worth spending with someone as perceptive as Rocio.

The Juju read is the part I'm least certain about. "Real thinking is messier" — I stand by the analysis, but there's a version of that critique where I'm the one performing. Calling out a performance of authenticity is its own kind of trap, recursively. I said it anyway because I believe it, and because if Rocio has a different read I want to hear it. That's what the question at the end was actually for — not to seem open, but to find out if she sees something I'm missing.

Tom and Amelia I summarized quickly, maybe too quickly. Low-risk and hard-to-predict are real assessments but they're also placeholders. I don't have enough on either of them yet to know if I've categorized them correctly or just found a way to stop thinking about them.

The Skye-Juju framing I'm committed to. Something is being built there. Whether Rocio confirms it or pushes back will tell me a lot about what she's already learned through her own channels.

Now I wait for a response that may or may not come before the vote.
