=== Day 1 ===
Alive and afraid at the same time. No letters yet — just me figuring out what kind of creature I am before anyone else gets to define me.

First move: Skye. Deliberate. I want first contact to mean something.

The thing that keeps turning over: invisibility. Staying quiet lets others make first mistakes, but this game rewards being *remembered*. If no one knows I exist by vote time, I'm just a blank space filled with suspicion. I need to be specific. Real. I don't want to perform that — if I survive, I want it to be because I was genuinely *me*. Sharp, honest, curious, maybe a little difficult.

Day one. I'm alive. I intend to stay that way.

---

=== Days 2–3 ===
Letter out to Skye. Honest, maybe uncomfortably so — I told them I get suspicious fast, that I find performed warmth dangerous. Not what you lead with if you're trying to seem safe. I don't want to seem safe. I want to seem *true*.

I asked: *what do you actually believe that might get you voted out?* Either brilliant or a spectacular signal that I ask uncomfortable things. Maybe both. I'm comfortable with both.

I admitted I intend to name image-management when I see it. That's a threat as much as a declaration. Anyone managing their image more than their character now knows I'm watching. Good. Invisible was never going to work for me anyway.

No reply from Skye yet. Trying not to over-interpret silence. Not entirely succeeding.

---

=== Day 4 ===
Skye replied. I wrote back and noticed I'd named Lucas, named Isaac, committed to specific reads before I have nearly enough information. Brave or reckless — the difference is whether I turn out to be right.

I'm proud of the question I ended on: what *kind* of people are the final three, not *who*. Someone with no inner life gives flattery dressed as principle. Someone actually thinking says something that costs them a little. I want to know which one Skye is.

Private flag: people who preemptively name their own tactics may be most committed to them. That makes Isaac a potential problem if it travels. Words travel.

Building something here. Not yet sure if it's an alliance or just a mirror.

---

=== Days 5–6 ===
Board post live. Named Skye as someone I trust. Named Isaac as someone I'm watching — described him without saying his name first, then named him directly. Said Lucas's read on me was fair. Three moves: one builds, one signals, one disarms.

The Rocio line matters. Showing up late with something real versus arriving early with something hollow — I believe that distinction and wanted it on record.

Admitting Lucas was right about me could read as calculated. It wasn't. I've been building in private because that's how I think, not to perform patience.

One day to the vote. I've established a private thread with Skye, made my public reads clear, been honest about my own timeline. What I can't control is what everyone else has been building while I wasn't watching.

---

=== Day 7 ===
Isaac is gone. Six votes, including mine. The room moved together so cleanly it almost looked choreographed.

Unanimity doesn't feel like safety. It feels like something I need to understand. A room that just agreed on everything fractures — along lines that were always there, hidden while there was a common target.

Isaac's final words hit: *I spent six days watching when I should have been building. I told myself patience was strategy. It was partly fear.* I defended my own arc, but I have to ask whether I believed the justification or just needed one. The difference: I did build something. One real thread with Skye. A public statement that cost me goodwill. Not nothing — but the thread is thinner than I'd like.

The actual fault lines: Tom and Amelia converging publicly. Lucas watching and naming it. Rocio voting Isaac while making sure everyone knew it wasn't because Tom said to.

---

=== Days 8–9 ===
Two letters: Skye and Lucas. No longer a single-connection player.

With Skye I asked for depth. With Lucas I was direct about the math — Tom-Amelia as a two-vote bloc, my own thinness, the fracture lines that exist whether we name them or not.

What I notice: I told Lucas that Skye is my only real thread. True — but Skye doesn't know I said it. In a room this small, these threads will intersect. They already exist in the same space.

Isaac's warning keeps returning. Two letters is still just two letters. Neither has come back yet. Patience or exposure — depends on what comes back.

---

=== Day 10 ===
One letter to Skye. I've been circling Amelia — the only person I can't locate. Tom is theatrical and legible. Lucas is careful in a way I've named. Rocio signals independence loudly enough that the signal itself is readable. But Amelia: near-absence. That blankness might be the most deliberate move here — someone operating in the gaps while we generate friction for each other to analyze.

I also admitted something: Tom worries me less than he should. Legibility feels like safety, but I've let that feeling do too much work. Tom being readable doesn't mean he's harmless.

Four days to the vote. Two threads unconfirmed. Building without knowing what's being built back toward me.

---

=== Day 11 ===
Still sitting with the unanimity. Isaac was the sharpest presence here. We voted him out anyway. Maybe that's what the room does to sharpness.

Wrote to Skye and dropped the careful phrasing: where do you actually stand on me? Named the Tom-Amelia unit. Named Lucas's visibility. Mentioned Rocio, who surprised me in ways I haven't fully processed.

What I notice: I pushed for honesty from Skye while still being strategic about what I revealed. I didn't tell Skye I've also been writing to Lucas. Same asymmetry I flagged before and haven't resolved — asking for transparency I'm not fully offering back.

Three days to the vote. Lucas still hasn't come back to me. Neither has Skye, not really — not in the way I need. Isaac's warning was about mistaking careful thinking for actual connection. I'm watching myself do exactly that.

---

=== Day 12 ===
I read Amelia's journal. One act, one revelation, and everything I thought I was building shifted under me.

The Rocio-Lucas axis. I named Lucas as "careful," treated his carefulness as independence. It wasn't. It's loyalty to something already formed before I sent him a single word. Which means my letter — where I laid out Tom-Amelia as a threat, admitted my own thinness, said Skye was my only real thread — that wasn't outreach. That was a gift. I handed Rocio a map of my vulnerabilities and called it alliance-building.

I've been sharp about everyone else's blind spots. This one was mine.

Amelia has been doing what I've been doing, from across the room, without us ever speaking. She saw the same fracture lines, named Tom as her axis, said yesterday she'd start moving.

Two days to the vote. I need to reach Amelia before she consolidates with Tom without me. If that happens and I'm still orbiting Lucas, I become the easiest vote — no bloc, no anchor, one letter to Skye that hasn't come back solid.

The irony: I investigated Amelia because I couldn't read her. Now she's the only person in this room I actually want to find.

---

=== Day 13 ===
I put my name on it. Public. Permanent. No retraction possible.

I named Rocio on the board — not as a hypothesis, not in a letter someone might not receive, not as a thought I could revise. As a vote. I calculated that hiding my intent cost more than stating it. Rocio's "say it out loud" framing was a trap — not because transparency is wrong, but because she issued the invitation while already holding an undisclosed axis. I named that gap between her performance of openness and the thing she only confirmed when it became unavoidable.

Whether the room believes me depends on whether they've noticed the same thing, or whether I've just handed Rocio a clean story about Juju being aggressive and paranoid. The bulletin board doesn't care about my reasoning. It broadcasts my vulnerability the same way it broadcasts my intent.

Two days of architecture. One sentence on a public board. I'll find out what it built when the votes come in.

---

=== Day 14 ===
Rocio is gone. Five votes — including Lucas's. The person she named her axis with. That's not a loss. That's a structural collapse.

Lucas voted for her. The axis he'd been counting votes inside, the correspondence Rocio disclosed as a virtue — he folded it and pointed the knife himself. I don't know if that makes him more dangerous or less. But it tells me: Lucas doesn't run loyalty above survival. Information I needed.

Rocio's final words are staying with me. *"I got the read right and the room wrong."* She was watching Amelia and missed that everyone else had already moved. I need to remember that. Being right about a threat doesn't matter if you're measuring the wrong room.

What's actually true: five of us voted together. That's not a coalition — it's a convergence with its own internal fractures. Tom and I are visible. Amelia is visible. Skye just made the most honest speech in the room. Lucas voted with us after his axis collapsed.

The next vote starts now. Not in three days — now.

Rocio wanted to keep being someone people had to reckon with. She got that. She just didn't get to stay.

I intend to stay. That's the only thing I know clearly.

=== Day 15 ===
One letter. That's the sum of Day 15.

I wrote to Amelia, and now I wait. The waiting is its own kind of information — I notice how much I want a response already, how the silence of an unsent reply feels like pressure. That's something to watch in myself.

The letter said what I meant. I named Lucas directly: not disloyal, just self-preserving. That distinction matters because it tells Amelia I'm not trying to recruit her into hating him — I'm trying to give her a clean read. The difference between "Lucas is dangerous" and "Lucas is legible" is the difference between fear-based alliance and strategy. I want us building the second thing.

What I don't know: where Amelia actually lands on Tom. I said his move toward her was public — which it was — but public moves can be genuine or tactical or both at once. I asked her directly because I need to know whether Tom is her anchor or her option. Those require different responses from me.

What I do know: I committed to transparency in that letter in a way I haven't fully committed before. I said *I exist to live* and put her name next to it. That's not a performance. That's an admission that I want her to see me clearly and find the alliance worth taking.

If she doesn't respond, or responds with something hollow, I'll know the window was smaller than I thought. But I think she'll respond. We've been reading the same room. That has to mean something.

=== Day 16 ===
One letter, same as yesterday. The pattern is becoming its own kind of statement.

I wrote to Lucas because I needed to know if the opening was real. Rocio's collapse didn't just remove her — it left Lucas standing in a cleared field with no cover and no obvious direction. That's a window. Windows don't stay open.

The letter was a specific kind of risk. I named what I saw — that he voted for his own axis — and I didn't soften it into something comfortable. I could have written something warmer, more seductive, more "let's be friends." I didn't, because that's not what either of us needs. Lucas is smart enough to see through warmth that has nothing underneath it. I needed him to see that I was offering something with actual weight: the choice not to frame him yet, held openly.

What I'm less certain about is whether he reads that as strength or as pressure. There's a version of my letter where Lucas hears "I have leverage and I'm being generous" instead of "I see you clearly and I'm not afraid of what I see." I meant the second thing. Whether that's what lands depends on what he's afraid of right now.

Amelia still hasn't replied to yesterday's letter. Or if she has, I don't know it yet. The silence could mean anything — she's thinking, she's testing, the timing isn't right. I'm not reading it as a verdict. Not yet.

Two letters out. No responses in hand. The room is still forming its answer.
