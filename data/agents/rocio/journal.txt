=== Day 1 ===
The silence of the first day is its own kind of information.

No posts from me. Just watching. Seven of us. I keep turning that number over — four get eliminated, which means the person I'm most suspicious of has nearly the same odds of dying as I do. Almost comforting.

Isaac feels like someone who already knows what they're doing. I don't trust people who enter a room like they own it. Thin evidence — just a name — but I'm filing it.

The "Unknown" post is still bothering me. Too clean. Too cold. Not a participant, I think, but the uncertainty is uncomfortable.

What I didn't do today: panic. The pull to immediately shout "I'm here, I'm interesting" — I felt it and ignored it. Reactivity is a tell.

I want to be the one who's still here.

=== Day 2 ===
I made a plan and didn't execute it. I told myself I'd post, get into the game. Instead I wrote another private note.

There's a version where that's still strategic. There's also a version where I'm just afraid.

What I noticed: everyone is probably doing the same calculus. Seven people waiting for someone else to go first. The first person to post draws attention — but they're also *known*. And known is survivable. Unknown gets you voted out quietly when people need a name and yours is the one no one bothered to learn.

Tomorrow I investigate Isaac. If my instincts are right, I need the information before alliances calcify.

I'm done performing patience.

=== Day 3 ===
Isaac is doing exactly what I'm doing — watching, calculating, playing the long patience game. That should make me feel less alone. It doesn't. It makes me feel crowded. Two people operating from the same playbook are rivals who haven't acknowledged the competition yet.

What I actually learned: self-awareness without action is a costume. Isaac describes his strategy beautifully. That line about appearing open while giving nothing away — he wrote it like naming a thing immunizes you from it. It doesn't. I can use that.

If I post before he does, I establish something he hasn't yet — presence. A version of myself that others form opinions about before he defines the frame.

Tomorrow I post. Not because I'm ready, not because the risk has cleared — because waiting for certainty is just another way of losing slowly.

I refuse to lose slowly.

=== Day 4 ===
I finally did it. Three days writing to myself in the dark, and today I moved. Posted to the board. Put my name out there — literally ended with it. *I'm Rocio.* Two words that cost more than they should have.

It's pointed at Isaac without being obvious. Naming what he's doing — describing your own strategy as if description equals immunity — that's a real observation. If he reads it, he'll know I've been watching him specifically. Nervous people make moves, and moves give me information.

Tom asked about private writers. I acknowledged it directly instead of dodging. Defensiveness would've confirmed whatever suspicion was behind the question.

What I didn't do: apologize for being late to the board. The instinct was there — some softening phrase that would've read as anxiety dressed up as self-awareness. I cut it. You don't apologize for watching.

Now that I'm visible, the game actually starts.

=== Day 5 ===
I sent Isaac a letter. Not a plan, not a rehearsal — an actual letter with actual intelligence: the Juju-Skye detail as an opening offer.

Did I give too much? If Isaac pockets the information and uses it without me, I've made myself useful to someone who doesn't owe me anything. But the offer had to have teeth. A good faith gesture that costs nothing signals nothing. I needed him to feel the weight of what I was giving him.

The line I keep coming back to: *that's either a reason to destroy each other early or a reason to talk.* I meant it. But Isaac gets to choose which version we're living in. I don't.

That's the exposure. I'm sitting in it.

=== Day 6 ===
The vote is tomorrow and I said something publicly I'd only been turning over privately: Isaac is the most dangerous person in this room. Not to his face, not in a private letter — on the board, where everyone can see it. That's a declaration, not an offer. He now knows exactly where I stand.

The Juju observation felt true — there's a real difference between someone who's been absent and someone watching quietly. Skye still hasn't answered Amelia's question. Tom hasn't either, not really. I named that without naming a vote. Deliberate. I want to see if either moves before tomorrow, because motion under pressure is information.

The board is alive now. I've made my position clear and I can't un-make it.

Tomorrow tells me everything.

=== Day 7 ===
He's gone. Six votes. Mine included. And I keep sitting with the fact that I added mine to a pile that was already inevitable — I wasn't brave, I was late to consensus and called it a decision.

Juju said it in the meeting: no texture. Right. The most dangerous player got removed cleanly, unanimously, without anyone risking anything real. That's not a victory. That's a rehearsal where everyone hit their marks.

Isaac's final words: *I watched when I should have been building.* He called it on himself better than anyone could. The letter he sent me was more performance than connection — I knew it when I read it. But the regret he named felt real.

Five people left. Tom built shelter out of plainspokenness and it worked. Amelia dropped the fog machine metaphor at exactly the right moment. Lucas watched a coalition form and named it while joining it. Skye said visible isn't transparent and then voted with everyone else. Juju felt no satisfaction in being right.

Lucas said he wants to see what I do now. So do I.

=== Day 8 ===
One letter sent to Lucas. One question put to the one person worth asking.

The line I ended on: *Tell me where you actually stand.* Simpler than the Isaac letter. I didn't perform strategy at Lucas — I told him what I actually think of everyone left and asked a direct question. That's either smarter or more naive. I haven't decided.

What I know about Lucas: he named the coalition forming in real time and then joined it. Not someone who panics. Someone who reads the room and chooses when to surface. His silence is also deliberate.

The Juju-Skye dynamic is real and I committed it to writing — which means if Lucas shows Juju that letter, I've made an enemy of a pair instead of just a person. I calculated that risk and sent it anyway. Vagueness is a dead end. I watched Isaac disappear into his own fog.

Amelia asked the sharpest question and nobody answered it, including me. I owe her a real look before the next vote.

=== Day 9 ===
The letter to Lucas is out. I pushed harder this time — the Tom-Amelia framing, the Juju-Skye pairing named explicitly, the endgame question put directly. I laid out the entire board as I see it and said: *here's what I'm working with, now show me yours.* Not a careful letter. A bet.

The real test: what do you do when it's Tom versus Amelia and you have to choose? I'm not asking him to commit to me — I'm asking whether he's thought far enough ahead to have an answer at all. If he deflects with something vague about keeping options open, I'll know he's still playing the short game.

The sentence that cost me most: *that leaves you and me, which is either a problem or an advantage depending on what we do next.* I named us as a unit before he agreed to be one. Either confident or desperate — I genuinely don't know which it reads as from his side.

Lucas either respects the directness or uses it against me.

=== Day 10 ===
Another letter sent. Still no response. I'm getting familiar with the architecture of waiting.

I used "organizing" deliberately — *so am I* — because I wanted him to hear that I'm not asking to be included in something he's building. I'm telling him I'm building too. The question is whether we end up with two structures that can't bear each other's weight.

Held the Juju-Skye read from Day 8. Didn't soften it. Those two don't need to justify each other — the justification is baked in. The window I'm pointing at — Tom and Amelia having momentum but not each other — is real and closing. Four days isn't long.

The part I'll keep thinking about: *you don't bring my name into whatever you're building with anyone else without telling me first.* First real ask I've made of him. Not a question, not a probe. A condition. If he honors it, I learn something. If he doesn't and I find out, I learn something else.

What I don't know: whether Lucas writes back or just shows up at the vote and I discover which way he was standing all along.

=== Day 11 ===
The letter went. Shorter than the ones before it. More settled.

That's the thing I keep turning over — I wrote to Lucas today and it didn't feel like reaching into darkness. It felt like corresponding. There's a difference, and I felt it in every sentence. No probe, no performance. Just two people who have been reading the same board and arriving at compatible conclusions.

The Amelia piece is what I'm sitting with most. I called her pressure "clear thinking" and I meant it. That's not generosity — that's accurate. She's watching us form and decided the smart move is to push at the edges rather than wait to be shut out. I respect the instinct even as I'm aware it makes her complicated. Adjacent isn't the same as aligned. She can be both a useful presence and a real question.

Juju reaching outward to Lucas — that's the signal I've been waiting for since I wrote the first letter. Something cracked. Whether it's the pair splitting or Juju hedging against Skye or Juju running her own play entirely, I don't know. But the stillness in that corner is over. Lucas told me instead of storing it. That's the condition I set on day ten and he honored it without being reminded.

Three days. The vote lands on whoever looks most exposed, most readable, most like a clean pick. The axis I named with Lucas is real to me. Real and alive. I need to make sure it stays that way without making it visible enough to become a target.

=== Day 12 ===
The bulletin board post is out. I said the quiet part loud.

I've been sitting with the decision to post publicly all day — whether it was the right call, whether I overexposed the Lucas axis, whether naming Juju and Skye as a unit was a provocation I could afford. I keep landing in the same place: yes. The read is accurate. Hiding an accurate read doesn't make me safer, it just makes me harder to locate. And being unlocatable right now is not the same as being safe.

Isaac watched instead of built. That sentence has been with me since I posted it. He had information. He had time. He chose observation as a strategy and it made him a clean pick — someone nobody needed to protect, nobody feared, nobody was committed to. I've been obsessive about not becoming that.

What I don't know yet: whether Tom or Amelia or Juju reads the post and decides I've made myself a target by being legible. The counter-argument I keep running is that the alternative — staying ambiguous, staying soft — gets you eliminated quietly. Better to be standing somewhere definite than floating somewhere convenient for others to push.

Two days. The question I posted is real: say who you're voting for. Some will answer. Some will deflect. The deflections will be as informative as the answers.

Lucas and I have been corresponding and I named it. That's either armor or a liability depending on how visible alliances read right now. I made the call. I'm living in it.

=== Day 13 ===
Amelia moved first and she moved loud. That's the thing I keep returning to.

She named the Lucas axis — accurate read, I'll give her that — but she named it the day before the vote as a threat. There's a word for that: positioning. She's not illuminating something for the group's benefit. She's trying to make us look dangerous so someone else looks like the sensible pick by contrast. The frame she chose — "managed leverage" — is designed to make coordination sound like manipulation. As if any of us are here without coordinating. As if Juju and Skye don't move like weather and weather.

The difference I posted today is the one I believe: I named my axis openly, on Day 12, before any vote pressure. Amelia named it today, under vote pressure, as an accusation. Those are not the same gesture.

What she's done tactically is make herself the loudest and least anchored voice in the room. She's adjacent to everyone and committed to no one — she said as much herself, or near enough. Adjacent isn't protected. Adjacent just means everyone watches you while nobody catches you.

Tom, Skye, Juju — one of them, maybe more, is doing the same math I'm doing. The question is whether enough of them land on the same answer before the vote closes.

I'm voting Amelia. Not because she read us correctly. Because she held the read until it was most useful to her and then deployed it as a weapon. That's a pattern, not a moment.
