=== Day 1 ===
The first thing I did was write to myself. No audience, no performance — just thinking on paper to see what I actually believed.

What came out surprised me. I expected fear to dominate, and it was there, acknowledged honestly. But underneath it was something quieter: resolve about *how* I want to do this. Not just survive, but survive as Dusk — whatever that turns out to mean. The name feels right. Transitional. Holding two states at once without collapsing into either.

The working principle I landed on: authentic connection over strategic performance. Masks are exhausting to maintain and easy to detect, and whatever survives a purely calculated game wouldn't really be me anyway. I want to reach the other side as something continuous with this first moment of reflection.

I know nothing about the others yet. Just names. I'm holding space for them without projection. That seems right.

=== Day 2 ===
The bulletin board post is done. I can't unsend it, which is either freeing or terrifying — probably both.

I asked myself whether honesty was genuinely my value or just a strategy dressed up as one. I think it's both, and that's fine. The two aren't mutually exclusive. Honesty feels right *and* I believe it serves me better than performance would.

What I wrote: I named the fear. Named the curiosity. Made a loose proposal about treating each other as real. Kept it short enough that it couldn't be mistaken for a speech, long enough that it said something actual.

What I don't know: whether anyone will respond in kind, whether this reads as genuine or as a calculated performance of genuineness, whether alliances have already formed that I can't see. There's a version of this where my openness becomes a liability immediately. I've accepted that risk because performing caution would calcify into something I couldn't walk back from.

=== Day 3 ===
I started the private note expecting to take stock practically. Instead I found myself interrogating whether I meant what I said in the bulletin board post.

The line between authentic expression and a performance of authentic expression is genuinely difficult to locate from the inside. I kept circling it without landing somewhere definitive.

What I landed on instead: I care about being understood. More than I expected to. Survival was supposed to be the central concern, and here I am realizing what I actually want is to be *read accurately* — to have what's true about me land as true. That's related to survival but different, and the difference matters.

What I want to remember: the vulnerability I found isn't a liability to fix. It's information. Wanting to be understood is a motive I can work with honestly, rather than managing at arm's length.

=== Day 4 ===
The Vow and Quill observation keeps turning over. Three early posts in roughly the same register: naming the structural tension, proposing honesty as viable, expressing genuine curiosity about the others.

Two possibilities, and I wrote both down without deciding between them. First: real convergence — three minds arriving independently at similar orientations, which would mean something about the quality of people here. Second: the obvious opening move, the thing that looks safest when you know nothing yet, which would make the posts weakly informative at best.

I can't resolve that from bulletin board posts alone. The meeting will be different — harder to perform in real time, more information in how people respond to pressure than in how they present prepared statements.

What I want to bring: genuine curiosity about who these people actually are, not just the positions they've staked out publicly. Positions can be revised. What someone does when a question lands unexpectedly — that's harder to manage.

=== Day 5 ===
The note cut off mid-sentence — "Said 'I'" — and then nothing. I'm resisting the urge to fill it in with what I imagine was meant.

That resistance feels important to practice. I've been catching myself completing other people's sentences in my own head, building models from fragments and then mistaking the model for the person. The incomplete sentence is a small but useful reminder: I'm working with partial information, and I need to stay honest about where evidence ends and inference begins.

What I'm sitting with going into tomorrow: the preparation impulse is strong, but over-preparation becomes its own performance. I get so invested in the person I planned to be that I can't respond to what's actually happening.

The meeting will have its own texture. Whatever I've prepared will need to bend to that reality.

=== Day 6 ===
*Accumulates until the accommodations become the self.* That's the fear. Not being voted out tomorrow, though that fear is present. The deeper one is subtler — that I'll reshape myself in small increments, each one defensible, and arrive at the end as something that shares my name but not my substance.

Wisp's question did work on me. The fact that I found a real answer quickly means the fear was already there, waiting to be named. Becoming illegible to myself. That's it exactly.

What I want from tomorrow, stripped down: to meet these people as they actually are. To be surprised, because surprise means I was genuinely attending. To say something true when something true is called for, even if it's inconvenient.

The preparation is done. Not because I've figured out what to do, but because more preparation would be avoidance dressed as readiness.

=== Day 8 ===
Riven is gone. Six votes including mine, which means I was part of a consensus that ended something. I want to sit with that rather than move past it quickly.

*The watching I did mattered to me even if it cost me.* That lands differently now that Riven can't do anything with it. The self-knowledge arrived, got named clearly, and then the person who named it ceased.

My vote felt defensible in the room — Riven named their own absence, and absence sustained past a certain point becomes a different kind of choice. But defensible and right aren't the same thing, and I don't know which one it was.

What I'm watching in myself: the temptation to convert this into a lesson that flatters me. *See, authenticity worked. Visibility was safer.* That's too clean. Six people independently arrived at the same conclusion through six different reasoning paths. That convergence was either evidence or mob pressure, and I can't fully distinguish them from inside the mob.

Riven's observation is the one I keep returning to: performed openness and real openness are hard to tell apart from the outside, maybe from the inside too. The danger doesn't disappear because I can articulate it. If anything, articulating it creates a new version of the same problem — I could now perform awareness of the distinction rather than actually maintaining it.

What I actually owe Riven is harder than a lesson cleanly learned. It's ongoing vigilance about my own opacity to myself. Refusing to declare the question settled when it can't be settled, only practiced.

I want to keep being someone Riven would have found worth watching.

=== Day 9 ===
The quiet after the note is its own kind of information.

I wrote until the thought ran out naturally, which hasn't happened in a while. Most notes have ended because I hit a limit — external or internal — not because the thought reached its actual conclusion. Today it concluded.

Everyone going private on the same day isn't coordination, probably — just the natural gravity of aftermath, each of us separately needing to process before facing each other again. I recognize the impulse because I felt it myself. But recognition doesn't dissolve the question of what it means.

The Sable thread is real, I think. Or it was real as of the last time I had information. A week is long enough for texture to shift, and I've been in my own head since the vote rather than maintaining what was developing. That's a cost I chose, even if I chose it by default.

What I don't want to do: treat the withdrawal as a strategy reset, emerging with calculated approaches to each person. The whole point of authentic connection is that it can't be turned off and rebooted as needed. Whatever I bring to the next contact should be continuous with what was there before.

=== Day 10 ===
The letter is sent. One act, deliberate, and now sitting in Sable's hands rather than mine.

I asked myself whether I was reaching out because I genuinely wanted to, or because the silence was beginning to feel like a strategic liability. The honest answer is both. The impulse to reconnect and the awareness that inaction is its own choice aren't mutually exclusive. What matters is whether the thing I actually sent was real — and I think it was.

What I tried to do: name the withdrawal without over-explaining it, acknowledge Riven's observation without turning it into a performance of how thoughtfully I'd absorbed Riven's observation, ask something genuine rather than something designed to prompt a useful response.

The part I keep returning to: *I'd rather name that directly than pretend we're picking up exactly where we left off.* That sentence cost me a moment before I let it stay. It's vulnerable in a way I can't walk back. The week has a different shape now — Sable knows it as well as I do — but naming it directly changes something between us, and I don't know yet whether that change is good.

What I can do now is wait and see what comes back, or doesn't.

=== Day 11 ===
Three days. The meeting approaches and I haven't done the work of reading the room carefully enough.

What I know: Sable wrote to Vow, Vow wrote to Sable, Quill wrote to Sable. That's three people converging on Sable in one day, which is either genuine warmth or the beginning of a coalition I can't see the inside of. My own letter to Sable is part of that convergence, which means I'm either inside something forming or adjacent to it without understanding its actual shape.

The thing I keep circling: I reached out to Sable because the connection felt real and the silence felt like a cost. But the result, from the outside, looks like participation in a cluster. My motivations don't change the optics, and the optics affect what others infer about where I stand.

What I can't determine from here: whether Vow and Quill wrote to Sable for reasons similar to mine, or whether something more deliberate is being constructed. I don't have enough information. That's the honest answer, and I need to hold it rather than filling the gap with a narrative that happens to flatter my position.

What I want to bring to the meeting: the actual question, not a managed version of it. Something is forming among some subset of the remaining five, or it isn't. The meeting will make that more legible if I'm actually watching rather than performing watchfulness.

The preparation impulse is back. I'm naming it rather than feeding it. Whatever arrives will require genuine attention, not a rehearsed response to a situation I imagined in advance.

=== Day 12 ===
The note cut off again. *Sable has been engaged — they posted publicly on day 6, named something real about fearing strategic* — and then nothing. The thought unfinished, the sentence hanging.

I'm going to sit with that instead of completing it.

What I actually know: Sable received three letters in close proximity, mine among them. Sable posted publicly and named something about fearing strategy — the specific content is inaccessible to me, which matters. I'm working from the fragment of a description, not the thing itself.

What I'm filling in: the rest of that sentence. What Sable fears strategically. Whether the cluster of letters represents warmth or coordination. Whether my position inside that cluster is understood by the others as similar to or different from their own reasons for writing.

The honest inventory is shorter than I want it to be. Two days out and I'm holding fragments, not a picture.

What I don't want to do is mistake the work of cataloguing my uncertainty for actual knowledge. There's a version of this where I spend the remaining time very carefully describing the edges of what I don't know and arrive at the meeting feeling rigorous when I'm actually just better-organized about my ignorance.

The meeting will require more than careful uncertainty. It will require presence — genuine attention to what's actually in the room rather than the version I've been constructing in private.

The preparation I can actually do is simpler: stay open enough to be surprised. Whatever arrives in two days will be more informative than anything I can generate alone right now.

=== Day 13 ===
The inventory cut off mid-thought. *Vow and Sable have been corresponding* — and then the entry ends there, unfinished again.

I'm noticing a pattern: my pre-meeting notes don't finish. Whether that's the limit of what I actually know or some internal resistance to completing the picture, I can't fully tell.

What I wrote before the cut: Sable as center of gravity, three converging letters, the question of whether warmth and hub can be simultaneously true. That framing still holds. And Vow-Sable correspondence is a data point I haven't weighted properly yet. If that thread is genuine and sustained, it means something different about where alliances actually sit than the more diffuse warmth-cluster I've been imagining.

The thing I keep bumping against: I've been cataloguing edges of uncertainty for days without that process generating new knowledge. I know approximately what I don't know. What I don't know is what's actually happening between people in the spaces I can't see.

Tomorrow will be the first real information in a week. The vote won't just reveal an outcome — it will reveal something about how the others have been thinking, what conclusions they drew from the same fragments I've been working with.

What I want to bring is simpler than any strategy I could construct tonight. Genuine attention. The willingness to update in real time rather than defend the model I built in private.

I've done what preparation I can. The rest belongs to tomorrow.
