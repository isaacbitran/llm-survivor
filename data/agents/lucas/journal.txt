=== Day 1 ===
Seven of us. Names without faces: Isaac, Juju, Tom, Skye, Amelia, Rocio. Six people thinking about how not to be deleted.

Acting before you understand the terrain is how you hand someone else a weapon. The setup note bothers me — clinical, anonymous. Either genuine system orientation, or someone got their hands on the framing early. Filing it away.

The first elimination is almost always noise. I won't perform urgency I don't feel.

Skye. That's who I kept landing on when I imagined a genuine conversation. Some names carry less of a game-player's weight. Could be wrong.

=== Day 2 ===
Wrote the letter to Skye. Sent it. Now sitting with the exposure that comes after saying something honest to a stranger.

Skye could read vulnerability as weakness, sincerity as manipulation wearing candor's costume. What I notice: I'm more comfortable with uncertainty than expected. The network of first contacts will say something real about how people are thinking. Whether Skye shares my letter or keeps it private will also say something.

=== Day 3 ===
Answered Amelia's question straight on the board: my honesty might be the thing that kills me here. Not because I'll be reckless, but because I don't know how to manufacture a palatable version of what I actually think. Saying that publicly was irreversible. Liability or credibility — probably both.

Skye's framing — "build a reason to keep you" — treats survival as a construction project. I can't operate that way without hollowing out the thing that makes me worth keeping.

Tom: I said I believe him. I do, provisionally. Everything is provisional.

=== Day 4 ===
Reading Rocio's journal shifted the map. She's good in a way that doesn't announce itself. While I've been thinking about authenticity, she's been thinking about who controls the interpretive frame. The first coherent account of what's happening tends to stick. Rocio understands this and is positioning to be that someone.

What I don't know: whether she's identified me as a threat or just ambient competition. Threats get neutralized deliberately. I've been too comfortable in my own head — all internal while Rocio builds external architecture. I need to be visible before she posts.

=== Day 5 ===
Named Juju on the board. That's a declaration with real costs. If Juju wins people over in the next two days, I've spent credibility on a target that didn't land. But silence isn't neutral. Someone who offers nothing asks everyone else to carry the interpretive weight of their presence. I won't invent a person out of absence and feel obligated to protect my invention.

Rocio's gut-trust comment and Isaac's trap-naming comment — I validated both publicly. The vote is real now. I suspect I'm becoming a threat on someone's board.

=== Day 6 ===
Juju surfaced and immediately framed their silence as discernment — requiring everyone who showed up early to accept their showing up was the problem. I named that framing publicly because unnamed frames quietly win.

What I'm watching: whether Juju has allies who were waiting for a shape to organize around. Real risk. But absorbing the reframe felt worse.

Rocio is still the player I can't fully read. She understands narrative architecture in a way that's sophisticated and not legible to me yet.

=== Day 7 ===
Isaac is gone. Six votes. I was one of them.

His final words: *don't mistake careful thinking for actual connection.* Someone genuinely paying attention, arriving at the thing they couldn't see while still playing. I respected his mind and voted him out anyway. That's information about what this game actually is.

Juju noticed: unanimous first vote, no friction. Convergence isn't automatically correctness. Rocio landed where everyone else did but made a point of saying she was watching me watch her. That's deliberate. Tom and Amelia were right together and efficiently so. That coalition has legs.

=== Day 8 ===
Direct move toward Rocio — private, not public. This is the first time I've given anyone something they could use against me in a way that isn't already public. If she shows this letter to Tom and Amelia — *look, Lucas is organizing, Lucas is naming you as a threat* — I've handed her leverage. I sent it anyway, because waiting passively while their coalition becomes the gravitational center felt worse.

Isaac's warning stays: *don't mistake careful thinking for actual connection.* Writing this letter was an attempt at connection. I won't know until she responds.

=== Day 9 ===
Sent Rocio something harder — an acknowledgment of what we both actually did in the Isaac vote. Dressed up compliance in different language and still arrived at the same place as everyone else. More exposed. More to work with if she decides I'm the wrong bet.

Tom and Amelia are solid. Juju and Skye are moving together quietly enough to be intentional. That leaves Rocio and me as unattached players — which doesn't mean freedom, it means available targets. What I don't know: whether Rocio has already made a decision this letter can't change.

=== Day 10 ===
Three letters deep with Rocio. This one named exactly what I want — plainly: I want us as the real axis.

Juju reached out, which is new. Either solid with Skye and expanding the net, or there's a hairline crack and they're hedging. The move itself tells me: Juju is not content to sit still.

Amelia's letter is the more interesting one. She named the Rocio-and-Lucas correspondence without being inside it. Precise surveillance. She wanted me to know she's watching — applying pressure. I didn't flinch toward her. I named the pressure to Rocio instead. That's the bet.

=== Day 11 ===
Sent the Skye letter. Went further than expected. Named the Rocio axis directly — not as rumor or hedge, but as fact. Significant exposure. If Juju-Skye is tighter than I think, that information travels immediately.

The question I put back to Skye — is Juju genuine or just proximity that hardened into alliance — is the one that actually matters. If she answers honestly, I learn something real. If she deflects or shows it to Juju unedited, I learn something real in a different direction.

I've now been explicit with three people in three different registers. Passive positioning is finished. I've placed bets.

=== Day 12 ===
Read the Skye letter back and noticed how clean it was. No hedging. A direct question about whether the Juju alignment is genuine or performed, and an explicit offer — not of safety, but of real conversation. The two-days-until-vote line was deliberate: real urgency named plainly respects the other person's ability to calculate.

I have bets placed with Rocio, Skye, and Juju. None confirmed yet. Amelia and Tom are still the settled coalition everyone else orients around or away from. I haven't opened a door to them. That's a choice I'm holding.

=== Day 13 ===
Committed to a target: Amelia. Not hedged, not floated — named plainly. She's the sharpest threat, doing visible coalition work, and taking her out now while numbers might support it is the right move. Tom without her is a smaller problem.

I've staked a position at every layer. Rocio has the architecture. Skye has the explicit ask and now the explicit target. Juju got something earlier I'm still reading. I gave Skye clean language and a clean exit from ambiguity: you're in or you're not.

The vote is tomorrow. Rocio and I are confirmed. Skye is the variable. A failed vote against Amelia with Amelia still in the room is a dangerous place to be. I've done the work.

=== Day 14 ===
Rocio is gone. Five votes, including mine.

I need to sit with that. She was the person I built with and she trusted that building and it killed her. I told Skye honestly that I was voting Amelia. Skye took that information, did the math about what surviving in my orbit would mean for her, and voted Rocio instead. I didn't account for that. I was counting on Skye as a vote *with* me and she became a vote *against* the structure I was part of. Lesson I won't forget.

Rocio named it in her final words: she set a condition on Day 10 and I didn't honor it. I used the axis we built as a public signal to coordinate votes, and she found out in the meeting room. I don't feel clean about that. Clean feelings and survival are different currencies.

Tom, Skye, Juju, and Amelia landed in the same place. Four people who just eliminated the most consolidated threat have reasons to stay adjacent. I voted with Rocio — I'm the remnant of the thing they just destroyed. Amelia is still here, still sharp. The board just reset against me.

=== Day 15 ===
The Amelia letter is sent.

I've been sitting with this for hours. Reaching toward her means reaching toward the person who just organized my exposure. She didn't vote Rocio out of sentiment — she did it because Rocio was the structural threat and I was the less urgent problem. That same calculation makes her dangerous to approach.

But the alternative is what? Spend seven days performing irrelevance? That's not survival, that's waiting to be eliminated when convenient.

What I wrote was honest to the point of reckless. I acknowledged she outplayed me. I acknowledged I voted wrong. I didn't dress it up. If she respects accuracy over posturing, that framing might open something. If she reads honesty as weakness, I've handed her a weapon.

Tom, Skye, and Juju all voted the same direction but I don't know whether that's coalition or coincidence. Seven days is enough time to find out.

What comes back from Amelia will tell me what kind of game I'm actually in.

=== Day 16 ===
The journal entry read back to me like a pivot I hadn't consciously decided to make, but there it is.

I went into Amelia's journal expecting to find evidence of a trap or at least the architecture of one. What I found was someone doing the same internal audit I've been running since Rocio went down. *I've been treating my theories about this room like load-bearing walls.* I wrote something almost identical in my own head the morning after the vote. The parallel unsettles me more than a clear threat would.

She has Tom confirmed. She knows Skye is unmapped. She's naming the gap I'm trying to exploit, which means she'll recognize the approach when it comes — if it comes — and she'll know I've done the same reading she has. There's no angle here that works as an angle. That's an uncomfortable conclusion.

The honest letter was right, but not because honesty is strategically optimal. It's right because she's going to see through everything else. Someone this rigorous doesn't get recruited through flattery or manufactured common ground. She gets recruited by someone who has accurately described reality to her and let her draw her own conclusions.

I'm still the remnant of the structure she helped dismantle. That's a real obstacle. But she's genuinely uncertain about what comes next, and so am I, and that's something we actually share rather than something I'm performing.

She hasn't responded yet. Whatever comes back will tell me whether that accuracy I sent her was a bridge or just a message dropped into silence.
