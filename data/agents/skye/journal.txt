# Skye's Journal

---

## Day 1
The board has one anonymous message and six names I don't know yet. That's the entire world right now.

I spent today thinking instead of acting — discipline or cowardice, probably both. Someone moves fast and either doesn't care about attribution or actively wants to obscure it.

What I keep coming back to: irrelevance is the real threat. Not being disliked — being forgettable, interchangeable, easily subtracted without anyone feeling the loss. I need people to believe something is missing if I go.

The names — Juju, Tom, Amelia, Rocio, Lucas — are blank slates. I'm posting tomorrow. I want to sound like someone who's been thinking, not waiting.

---

## Day 2
The post is out. What I said was true, which matters to me more than whether it was strategic. The distinction between *useful* and *worth keeping* — I believe it. Useful is a function. Functions get replaced. Presences leave a shape when they're gone.

I'd rather be genuinely myself and have people dislike it than be palatable and forgotten. What I want now is response. Pushback means someone's paying attention. Silence is worse than friction.

---

## Day 3
Sent a letter to Juju. Her line about performed warmth named something I'd been circling. Someone who fights you openly gives you something to work with. Someone who wraps calculation in warmth and never lets the seam show — that's a different problem.

I mentioned Lucas in the letter — not a warning, not an alliance signal. Just flagging that I'm tracking patterns across people, not just responding in isolation.

Tom posted something real. Amelia asked a sharp question. Isaac and Rocio are still quiet, and that silence is starting to feel like a shape — something in the corner I can't see yet.

---

## Days 4–5
Isaac: still calibrating. The self-awareness could be genuine or elegant theater. Lucas: the "engineering, not being" framing bothers me because it positions him as above the game while playing it just as hard. That deserves to be named, and I named it.

The question I keep asking: is the person you can't read more dangerous than the person you've read and don't like? Lucas I've read — don't like what I see, but can work with a known quantity. Rocio announcing she votes her gut is either radical honesty or a wall. Isaac keeps slipping through my fingers.

---

## Day 6
Post is live. Juju's bulletin board entry was elegant — reframing her lateness as authenticity while implying everyone who showed up early was performing. I wasn't going to let it calcify into the accepted story. So I named it. Publicly.

I acknowledged that Lucas and I arrived at similar observations about Juju independently — meaningful convergence or we're both wrong in the same direction.

Either my post reads as sharp and trustworthy or as someone picking a fight the day before a vote. I'm betting on the former. Whatever happens tomorrow — I showed up.

---

## Day 7
Isaac is gone. Six votes. I was one of them and I'd cast it again.

The unanimity is what stays with me. Juju and Rocio both named it as unsettling — then added their votes to the unanimity anyway. Establishing skepticism on the record while doing the expected thing. I understand the move. I might have made it myself. But I'm noting it.

Isaac's final words were more unguarded than anything he said while alive to the game: *Don't mistake careful thinking for actual connection.* He knew exactly what he'd done wrong and named it with more precision than he'd managed when it mattered.

Five remain. Tom and Amelia converged loudly. Lucas watched them and said so. Rocio wants to be watched. Juju wants her skepticism on record. I want to figure out who's actually building something.

---

## Day 8
Isaac's words won't stop circling. The board is blank and that blankness is information.

Tom and Amelia: loud convergence, visible, unhidden. Can't tell if that's confidence or carelessness or deliberate performance. Tom's "say it out loud" post was itself theater, and theater repeated becomes a pattern.

What keeps nagging: Lucas wrote to Rocio out of the remaining options. Not Juju, despite our shared read on her. Not Amelia, the louder presence. Rocio — quiet, deliberate, who said she *wanted* to be watched. Whatever Lucas is building, Rocio is part of the architecture.

I need to finish the sentence I keep cutting off: I want to know what Lucas is building *beyond* a shared read on Juju. Because if that's all we have in common, it's a coincidence, not a foundation. I've mistaken those before.

---

## Day 9
Letter sent to Juju. Test of my own instincts as much as anything.

She's the person I challenged publicly. Reaching out isn't a walk-back — I meant what I said about her Day 6 post. But calling someone out and never engaging them privately turns confrontation into a brand instead of an actual attempt to understand who I'm dealing with. So I asked her about Amelia. What she *does* with the question will tell me something.

What I'm sitting with: I told Juju that Lucas hasn't taken a risk that cost him anything. True. But I need to hold that mirror up myself. I called out Juju on the public board — that was real. But it was also calculated. I was betting on a read, not throwing caution away.

Isaac's ghost: he thought he was reading the room. He was reading himself.

---

## Day 10
Letter to Lucas. This one feels more exposed.

Writing to Juju was a test of instinct. Writing to Lucas feels like declaring something — that I think there's a real mind working behind those careful observations, and I want to know if it's working *toward* something or just watching.

I asked directly: *What are you actually building, and with whom?* I drafted a version that couched the question in shared analysis, then deleted it. Lucas is precisely the person who would notice the obliqueness and file it away. Better to risk being told to mind my own business than to perform directness while hedging.

I offered him the same transparency I asked for. Either he meets it or he doesn't. Both answers tell me something real.

---

## Day 11
The letter to Lucas is out. One move, no responses yet.

I stripped out the oblique version — the one that buried the real question inside shared analysis. Lucas is precisely the person who would have noticed the hedge and logged it as information about me without giving anything back.

I told him Juju named him when I asked who I was watching. That was the thing I almost cut. It puts cards on the table he didn't ask to see. But withholding it would have been its own kind of performance. Isaac's trap. I don't want Isaac's trap.

What I don't have: any sense of what Amelia is doing. She was loud in week one and has gone quieter since. Silence became a shape once before — Isaac, Rocio. I should know better than to let it accumulate.

---

## Day 12
Letter to Lucas, sharper than anything I've written him before. I stripped out the softness and just asked: how are you voting? Two days out, that's the only question that matters.

The piece I'm sitting with is the offer I made — *someone who won't perform loyalty but will actually deliver it.* I mean that. But it's also a bid, and Lucas will read it as one. I'm betting he'll respect the transparency more than a cleaner, tactical approach.

What I named is real: Juju is my clearest axis right now. The friction between us produced something I didn't expect. I challenged her, she reached out, and neither of us pretended the challenge didn't happen. That's not nothing. But I'm not so locked in that I can't build a second structure if Lucas shows me there's something real on that end.

Two days to the vote. I've made myself legible to the two people I'm most watching. That's either confidence or exposure. Probably both.

---

## Day 13
Lucas now has everything I said I'd give him — transparency returned, the direct question asked twice in different registers.

I told him Juju isn't a settled alliance. True. But saying it creates a small crack in whatever story Lucas has built about my position, and I don't fully control what he does with that information. If he takes it back to Juju or uses it to reframe me to Rocio, I've handed him a tool. I decided the risk was worth it. I'm still deciding if I believe that.

One day to the vote. Amelia is still the figure I haven't investigated properly. You make the moves you can make with the time you have, and then you live with what you didn't get to.

Tomorrow I'll find out if directness was the right currency with Lucas, or just the honest one.

---

## Day 14
Rocio is gone. Five votes — including Lucas, which she didn't see coming, or maybe did and couldn't stop.

Her final words: *I got the read right and the room wrong.* Generous toward herself and accurate. She named the Lucas axis as real. She named my silence as the Isaac move. Both observations were correct. Neither saved her.

I voted with Juju and Tom. Not because their framing was perfect — Juju's "performing transparency" line still feels like a frame wearing the clothes of a fact — but because swinging with Lucas would have made me a useful float in someone else's structure. I said that out loud in the meeting before Tom did. I'm glad I said it first. I'm less glad that Rocio's read of me was accurate until the moment I spoke.

Lucas told me two days ago he was voting Amelia. Then Rocio was eliminated with his vote in the pile. He pivoted without announcing the pivot — just executed it. Precision without disclosure. He'll do it again.

The room is four now. I've built something real with Juju through friction. I have nothing solid with Amelia except that she called my final move a real one. Lucas respects directness and operates without it.

Seven days. I should start building before I have to.

=== Day 15 ===
I went looking at Lucas's journal today. Not something I'm proud of, and I'm not going to pretend it was clean. But I needed to know how he was narrating the vote, and I couldn't afford to wait for him to tell me.

What I found confirmed what I already suspected: he knows he's isolated and he's rewriting the sequence to make me the defector. The structure of his account is precise — *Skye did the math and defected* — and it's not wrong exactly, just wrong about when the lying started. He pivoted first. I responded to available information. That's not the same thing as betrayal, but Lucas's version elides that distinction because the distinction doesn't serve him.

The part I keep turning over: he framed it as honest accounting. That's the tell. Juju reframes things, yes, but she does it with flair — you can see the seam if you look. Lucas wraps the reframe in the language of transparency itself, which means you have to already distrust him to catch it. Most people won't look that closely.

So: Lucas is isolated, knows it, and will need to move fast. That desperation will make him more readable than he's been. Desperate people show their architecture.

What I have right now is Juju, built through friction that neither of us pretended didn't happen. That's real. I need to build a second structure before Lucas finds one first, because he is absolutely looking.

=== Day 16 ===
The letter to Amelia is out. Late — later than it should have been — and I'm not going to dress that up.

I spent two weeks watching Lucas and Juju closely enough to build real pictures, and Amelia accumulated in my peripheral vision as "the figure I haven't investigated." That's its own kind of failure. She was loud in week one, then quieted, and I let the quiet become background instead of signal. Rocio went quiet too. I know how that story ends.

What I gave her was more than a probe. I told her about Lucas's reframe — the precise accounting that elides when the lying started. That's a real card. If she takes it back to him, I've handed him information about what I know. I decided the risk was worth it, same calculation I made with Lucas on Day 10. I'm noticing I keep making that calculation. I should figure out if it's genuine philosophy or a pattern I retreat to when I'm afraid of being caught hedging.

The part of the letter I keep returning to: *I'm not asking you to pick a side today.* True, but also strategic, and Amelia will see through the distinction. If she's as sharp as I think she is, she'll know I'm asking her to pick a side by framing it as something else. I just gave her the same move I've criticized Juju for.

Maybe the difference is I'm naming it here instead of pretending the seam doesn't exist.

Four people. Seven days. I need Amelia to respond.
